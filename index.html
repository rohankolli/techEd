AI guesses what comes next.

Most of what people call AI today is a very good guesser. Pick a sentence below and watch a toy model rank its next word.

The sky is …

    What AI is

    Artificial intelligence is software that learns patterns from examples instead of following rules a person wrote by hand. A spam filter that learned from millions of emails is AI. A calculator is not.

    Large language models, the kind behind chat assistants, learned from huge amounts of text. They don't look facts up in a database. They predict likely words, one after another, based on everything they saw in training.

    How it learns

    1. Collect examples. Photos labeled "cat" or "dog," or billions of sentences.
    2. Guess. The model makes a prediction, mostly wrong at first.
    3. Measure the miss. Compare the guess to the right answer.
    4. Adjust. Nudge millions of internal numbers so the next guess is a little better.
    5. Repeat. Do this an enormous number of times until the guesses get good.

    Where you already use it

    Your phone keyboard
    Suggests the next word as you type.
    Maps
    Predicts traffic and picks a faster route.
    Streaming apps
    Recommends shows based on what you and people like you watched.
    Hospitals
    Helps flag spots on scans for a doctor to review.

    Where it falls short

    It can sound sure and still be wrong. Predicting likely words is not the same as checking facts.

    It inherits bias. If the training examples were skewed, the output will be too.

    It doesn't know what it doesn't know. Anything after its training data, or outside it, is a blind spot.

    The habit worth building: treat AI output like a confident first draft from a stranger. Useful, worth checking.

    Glossary

    Model

    The trained program itself: a big set of numbers that turns inputs into predictions.

    Training data

    The examples a model learned from. Its quality shapes everything the model does.

    Neural network

    A model made of layers of simple math units loosely inspired by brain cells.

    Prompt

    The text you give a language model to respond to.

    Hallucination

    When a model states something false as if it were true.
